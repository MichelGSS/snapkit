# Tutorial: Record a short demo and turn it into a GIF

In this tutorial, you record a short screen video with sound, pause it in the middle, trim the result and turn the best part into an animated GIF. GIFs play everywhere, including in pull requests, chat apps and documentation sites that don't accept video.

**Time:** about 10 minutes
**You need:** SnapKit installed with its video component (included in the installer and in the zip). A microphone is optional.

## Step 1: Pick the area to record

1. Open the app you want to show.
2. Open SnapKit and select **Video** in the top bar.
3. Select the app window, or drag to select only part of it.
4. If you dragged an area, adjust it if you need to (drag the middle to move it or the handles to resize it), and then press **Enter** or select **Capture**. Selecting a window confirms it right away.

A small panel appears next to the area, and an orange frame marks what will be recorded.

## Step 2: Choose the sound

In the panel:

- Keep **System sound** selected to record what plays through your speakers or headphones.
- Select **Microphone** if you want to narrate.

## Step 3: Record

1. Select **Record**.

   The panel shows the elapsed time.

2. Do what you want to show. Speak if the microphone is on.
3. Select **Pause** whenever you need a break, for example to prepare the next part. Paused time isn't included in the video.
4. Select **Resume** to continue.
5. Select **Stop** when you're done.

SnapKit saves an MP4 file in your **Videos\SnapKit** folder and opens the **Recording saved** window.

## Step 4: Watch the recording

In the **Recording saved** window, select **Open** to play the video in your default player.

## Step 5: Trim the best part

1. Under **Clip**, set **Start** and **End** to the seconds you want to keep.
2. Select **Save only this clip**.

SnapKit creates a new video next to the original. The original isn't changed.

## Step 6: Create a GIF

1. In the same window, choose the **Frames per second** for the GIF. **12** is a good balance between smoothness and file size.
2. Choose the **Width**. **720 px** works well for documentation; smaller widths make smaller files.
3. Select **Create GIF**.

SnapKit converts the clip you chose in step 5 into a GIF, next to the video. When it's done, the window shows the file name.

## Step 7: Use the GIF

Select **Show in folder**, then drag the GIF into a pull request, a chat or a document.

## What you learned

- **Video** records an area of the screen as MP4, with system sound and microphone.
- You can pause and resume; paused time is left out.
- The **Recording saved** window trims clips and converts them to GIF without changing the original.
