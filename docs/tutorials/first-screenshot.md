# Tutorial: Take, annotate and share your first screenshot

In this tutorial, you capture part of your screen, point something out with an arrow and a label, hide a piece of private information and paste the result into a document. Along the way you meet the three things you'll use most in SnapKit: the region capture, quick annotation and the editor.

**Time:** about 10 minutes
**You need:** SnapKit installed ([Install SnapKit](../how-to/install.md)) and any app open on your screen, for example File Explorer or a web browser.

## Step 1: Start a region capture

1. Open the app you want to capture and leave it on the screen.
2. Press **Alt+Shift+S**.

   The screen dims slightly and the pointer becomes a crosshair. Nothing is frozen: videos keep playing and the clock keeps ticking. A small magnifier next to the pointer shows the pixels around it.

3. Move the pointer over different windows. SnapKit outlines the window under the pointer and shows its size.

> [!TIP]
> If **Alt+Shift+S** doesn't work, open SnapKit from the Start menu and select **Region** in the top bar. You can change the shortcut later in [Preferences](../reference/preferences.md).

## Step 2: Select an area

1. Drag from one corner of the area you want to the opposite corner.
2. Release the mouse button.

The area you selected stays exactly where it was, now framed, with a small toolbar below it. This is **quick annotation**: you annotate on top of the screen, without opening another window.

## Step 3: Add an arrow

1. In the toolbar, make sure **Arrow** is selected. It's the default tool for every new capture.
2. Drag from where you want the arrow to start to the thing you want to point at.

An arrow appears. If it's not quite right, press **Ctrl+Z** to undo it and drag again.

## Step 4: Add a label

1. In the toolbar, select **Text**.
2. Select a spot next to the arrow and type a short label, for example `Select this button`.
3. Press **Enter** to confirm.

## Step 5: Hide private information

If the area shows a name, an email address or anything else you don't want to share:

1. In the toolbar, select **Pixelate**.
2. Drag over the information you want to hide.

The text turns into large blocks that can't be read.

## Step 6: Copy the result

Press **Enter**.

SnapKit copies the annotated image to the clipboard, keeps a copy in its library and gets out of your way. The SnapKit window doesn't open.

## Step 7: Paste it into a document

1. Open the document, email or chat where you want the screenshot.
2. Press **Ctrl+V**.

The screenshot appears with your arrow, label and pixelation.

## Step 8: Find it again later

1. Open SnapKit from the system tray (the orange icon next to the clock) or from the Start menu.
2. Select **Library** in the top bar.

Your capture is at the top of the list, with a thumbnail. Select it to open it in the editor, where you can keep annotating or save it as a PNG file.

## What you learned

- **Alt+Shift+S** starts a region capture from any app.
- Dragging selects an area; selecting a window captures the whole window.
- Quick annotation lets you draw on the capture right where it is.
- **Enter** copies the result and closes; every capture is also kept in the library.
